# Vision

- **Status:** settled 2026-11-11 (forked from christ-online)
- **Owner:** Kinzey

## The problem

Blood on the Clocktower is a great game that is expensive, hard to get a copy of, and awkward to play without the physical box: it needs a grimoire, dozens of character tokens, reminder tokens, and a Storyteller who can track all of it. Playing with people who have never run a game is hard for the same reason.

## What this is

A phone-based companion for playing **in person**. Everyone has a phone; one person is the Storyteller. The app replaces the grimoire, the character sheet, and the tally sheet — not the conversation.

- **Storyteller-lite.** The app enforces the mechanics so that a first-time Storyteller cannot get the rules wrong: correct night order, illegal targets refused, vote thresholds computed, poisoning and ghost votes tracked. The Storyteller still makes every judgement call the rules leave to a human — what a poisoned player is told, who the Fortune Teller's red herring is. It does **not** run the game.
- **Trouble Brewing only**, at first. One script, 22 characters, the one every group starts on.
- **Shareable without accounts.** A game is a short code, a link, and a QR code. Anyone can start a game and hand out seats. No signup, no email, no password.

## What it is not

- **Not automated.** There is no bot Storyteller and no AI adjudicator. The judgement calls are the game.
- **Not a remote-play platform.** No voice, no video, no chat. Groups already use Discord or a dinner table.
- **Not a rules wiki.** It shows the rule at the moment it matters, in one sentence.
- **Not an account-based product.** No profiles, no history across games, no social features — until something genuinely needs them.

## Who plays

Small groups in a lounge room: 5–15 people, one of whom has played before. The tool is designed for the person who has played once but never story-told, because that is the person who owns the game and will be handing out the link.

## Scope ladder

Each rung is a playable release; nothing above the current rung is designed in advance.

1. **Deal and count.** Private roles, physical votes recorded by tapping seats, deaths announced. Playable with a Storyteller who knows the rules.
2. **Storyteller-lite.** Night wizard, night order, targeting constraints, poisoning, red herring, ghost votes, executed-character reveals. Playable by a first-time Storyteller.
3. **Persistence and review.** Event log, replay, post-game review, per-player game history by seat PIN.
4. **More content.** Sects & Violets, Bad Moon Rising, custom scripts — as data drops, not rewrites.
5. **Multi-device table.** A shared town-square display, plus travellers and fabled.

## Success test

A group that has never run the game before can play a full round of Trouble Brewing, start to finish, without anyone consulting the rulebook mid-game — and without anyone accidentally seeing something they shouldn't.
