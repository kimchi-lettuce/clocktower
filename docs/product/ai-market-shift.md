# AI market shift — what it means for ChristOnline

Research note, June 2026. Source: ["The AI Software Cope Situation Is Crazy"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n0uE5EzJpes) (12 min). Written from the perspective of a working SaaS owner. Claimed in the video, tested here against our situation — not established fact.

## The video's claim, in one line

AI makes software cheap to build. That **kills the feature moat** (anyone can copy your product in days instead of months) but **leaves four moats standing**: trust, maintenance/security/liability, distribution relationships, and incumbency.

## The claims, and how they apply to us

| # | Video claim | Impact on ChristOnline |
|---|-------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Product/IP moat gone — clone cycle 3–6 months → 3–6 hours | High. "All-in-one church management" as a feature list is our most copyable asset. ChurchSuite, Planning Center etc. already cover most features; AI narrows the gap further. |
| 2 | Cheap product → crowded market → distribution costs rise, competition becomes price wars | Medium. Same church channels (networks, conferences, word of mouth), more people bidding for them. |
| 3 | Trust is the winner's moat when a market floods | **Works for us.** Churches are extremely trust-sensitive (member data, children's data, safeguarding). Our real relationships + the warden-approved privacy governance work is trust a new AI-built competitor cannot quickly fake. |
| 4 | Maintenance, security, liability = the strongest remaining reason customers pay for SaaS | **Our best structural defence.** A church will not run safeguarding/member data on unaccountable software. Sell *accountability* — uptime, security, someone to blame — not features. |
| 5 | Sell to devs → they will build it themselves → get out | **Reversed favour.** Churches are non-technical buyers who will never build their own tools. The flood risk is other SaaS founders, not our customers self-building. |
| 6 | "Models can't do X yet" arguments are the weakest defence — models improve weekly | Caution for us: our engineering stack (typed backend, careful architecture) is a real *today* edge, but it must never be the strategy. The quality gap will shrink. |
| 7 | Innovator's dilemma — customers don't ask for what doesn't exist yet | Two edges. Incumbent church software is old and slow (vulnerable); but churches also adopt new things slowly (we must be patient, relationship-led). |
| 8 | Dropshipping lesson: low barrier to entry + no moat = race to the bottom | The trap to avoid: competing on features in a red ocean. |

## What follows for us

1. **Position on trust, not features.** Pitch: the governed, accountable system of record for church life (safeguarding, privacy, access control). "All-in-one" describes scope, never the selling point.
2. **Niche workflow first.** A defensible, governance-heavy workflow (welcoming / attendance / safeguarding) beats chasing ChurchSuite across everything. Depth in what incumbents do badly.
3. **Use AI hard for build speed; don't lean on it as an edge.** Cheap build suits our side-project economics. But differentiate with the things AI can't produce: church relationships, warden trust, accountability.
4. **Relationship-led distribution is the asset.** Daniel's and our existing church contacts are the un-copyable channel — invest there, not in paid ads against incumbents.
5. **Revisit if models close the trust-ops gap.** If AI can credibly run governance/compliance-grade systems on its own, moat #3 weakens too — watch for that and re-evaluate.
